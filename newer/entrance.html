How Jew-Hatred Evolves

Throughout history, Jew-hatred has evolved by adapting its accusations and justifications to the dominant moral and ideological values of each era.

The Three Eras of Jew-Hatred framework, developed under the scholarly leadership of Dr. Naya Lekht, a scholar and educator specializing in antizionism, examines this recurring pattern through three major historical forms: religious antijudaism, racial antisemitism, and national antizionism.

Building on earlier scholarship, most notably Rabbi Lord Jonathan Sacks's observation that Jew-hatred mutates across historical eras, Dr. Lekht's framework advances a central insight: in each era, Jews are cast not merely as outsiders, but as embodiments of evil, accused of violating the very virtues their societies hold most sacred.

Across these three historical eras, Jews have been portrayed as enemies of God, enemies of racial and national purity, and enemies of humanity. Although the accusations differ, each transforms Jews into moral villains through the prevailing moral language of its time.

The accusations change. The language changes. The justification changes. The pattern of demonization remains.

The Three Eras of Jew-Hatred framework illustrates how anti-Jewish demonization has been adapted to the dominant moral and ideological language of different historical periods. Religious antijudaism portrays Jews as violators of religious truth; racial antisemitism portrays Jews as threats to racial and national purity; and demonizing antizionism portrays the Jewish collective and its state as violators of human rights. These historical forms can overlap and coexist.

Understanding The Three Eras

01

Antijudaism: Religious

The Jew as the Enemy of God

Antijudaism is hostility toward Jews rooted in religious beliefs and accusations, particularly the portrayal of Jews as enemies of religious truth.

For centuries, anti-Jewish hostility was expressed primarily through religious accusations. In Christian Europe, Jews were portrayed as enemies of God and Christianity, accused of rejecting and killing Christ, desecrating sacred objects, poisoning wells, and murdering Christian children in fabricated blood libels.

These accusations transformed Jews into supposed enemies of the sacred, allowing persecution to be justified as a religious and moral obligation. Anti-Jewish discrimination also existed in parts of the Islamic world under different religious and political conditions.

02

Antisemitism: Racial

The Jew as the Enemy of Racial Purity

Racial antisemitism portrays Jews as an alien group threatening society's racial, cultural, and national integrity.

During the nineteenth century, racial theories, nationalism, and secular ideologies increasingly transformed older anti-Jewish prejudices into racial and political accusations.

The term “antisemitism” was popularized in 1879 by Wilhelm Marr. Jewish identity was increasingly recast as an inherited characteristic that could not be changed through religious conversion.

These ideas were reinforced through conspiracy theories, racial pseudoscience, and nationalist propaganda, culminating in the Holocaust — the systematic murder of six million Jews.

03

Antizionism: National

The Jewish Collective and State as Enemy of Human Rights

The Third-Era, antizionism, is a form of Jew-hatred when historical anti-Jewish libels and patterns of collective demonization are redirected toward Zionism, Jewish national identity, and the State of Israel.

In the twentieth century, older anti-Jewish accusations were increasingly adapted to the language of national liberation, anti-colonialism, racial equality, and human rights.

Antizionist ideas emerged in different political movements before Israel's establishment in 1948 and were later intensified through Soviet propaganda, particularly after 1967.

In its demonizing forms, antizionism portrays Israel and Zionists as embodiments of the world's gravest evils — racism, colonialism, apartheid, Nazism, and genocide. The framework distinguishes this demonization from criticism of specific Israeli government policies.

About The Three Eras Framework

The Three Eras of Jew-Hatred is an educational framework developed and taught by Dr. Naya Lekht, scholar and educator, examining the historical evolution of anti-Jewish demonization through religious, racial, and national forms.

The framework identifies a recurring historical mechanism: anti-Jewish hostility adapts its accusations to the moral and ideological language of its time.

These eras are not rigid chronological divisions. Religious, racial, and national forms of Jew-hatred can overlap, coexist, and reinforce one another.

About the Exhibition

The Third Era: Antizionism and the History of Jew-Hatred is a historical and educational exhibition presented by Stop Antizionism, an initiative of Cohesion Partnership.

Through historical archives, primary sources, powerful imagery, multimedia, and testimony, the exhibition explores the evolution of anti-Jewish demonization and examines how historical libels have been transformed and carried into the modern era.The digital exhibition is available online, with a physical exhibition planned for 2027.
